🍔 Starting a Restaurant Business

Starting a restaurant can be an exciting business opportunity, but it requires careful planning, budgeting, and preparation. This guide walks you through the major steps involved in starting and operating a restaurant.

Restaurant Business Guide

Thinking about starting a restaurant? This guide covers the important areas you should consider before opening, from planning and startup costs to staffing, marketing, and day-to-day operations.

📋 1. Plan Your Restaurant

Before spending money on equipment or signing a lease, decide what type of restaurant you want to operate.

  • Choose your restaurant concept.
  • Decide what food and drinks you will offer.
  • Identify your target customers.
  • Research your local competition.
  • Create an initial business budget.

💰 2. Understand Your Startup Costs

Restaurant startup costs can vary significantly depending on the location, size, concept, equipment, renovations, and whether you are opening a new location or taking over an existing restaurant.

Before signing a lease or making major purchases, create a detailed startup budget. Try to estimate each expense separately rather than using one general startup-cost number.

Common Startup Expenses

Some common one-time or initial expenses include:

  • Lease and security deposit
  • Renovations and improvements
  • Kitchen equipment
  • Furniture and fixtures
  • POS and payment systems
  • Initial food and beverage inventory
  • Licences and permits
  • Insurance
  • Professional and legal services
  • Website, branding, and signage
  • Marketing and advertising
  • Initial staff hiring and training
  • Initial working capital

📊 Don't Forget Monthly Operating Costs

Startup costs are only part of the financial picture. You also need to plan for the regular expenses required to operate the restaurant after opening.

Common monthly operating costs may include:

  • Rent and occupancy expenses
  • Food and beverage costs
  • Employee wages and payroll costs
  • Utilities
  • Insurance
  • Cleaning and maintenance
  • Software and payment processing
  • Marketing and advertising
  • Accounting and professional services
  • Equipment repairs and replacement
  • Supplies and packaging
  • Delivery and other service fees

💡 Plan for Unexpected Expenses

Opening a restaurant can involve unexpected expenses such as construction delays, equipment problems, higher renovation costs, permit delays, or lower-than-expected sales during the first few months.

Having working capital available can help the business handle these situations without putting unnecessary pressure on cash flow.

It's also a good idea to include a contingency amount in your startup budget for unexpected costs. The exact amount will depend on the size and complexity of the project, but the important point is to avoid using every dollar of your available capital before opening.

💰 Don't Spend Your Entire Budget Before Opening

One common mistake is using most of the available money on renovations, equipment, and furniture before the restaurant opens.

A restaurant may need cash after opening to cover payroll, inventory, rent, utilities, marketing, repairs, and other expenses while sales are still developing.

Your startup budget should therefore answer two separate questions:

  1. How much money do I need to open?
  2. How much money do I need to operate until the business becomes stable?

That second question is extremely important for a new restaurant.

📍 3. Choose the Right Location

Location can have a major effect on a restaurant's success. A good location should match your target customers, concept, budget, and expected sales. Before signing a lease, carefully evaluate the area and the property itself.

Important Location Factors

Consider the following factors when comparing potential locations:

  • Visibility and signage from the street
  • Customer and vehicle traffic
  • Parking availability and accessibility
  • The number and type of potential customers in the area
  • Nearby restaurants and competing businesses
  • Rent and other occupancy costs
  • Access to public transportation and major roads
  • Nearby offices, residential areas, schools, hotels, or attractions
  • Local population and customer demographics
  • Safety and overall condition of the surrounding area

👥 Make Sure the Location Matches Your Customers

The best location depends on the type of restaurant you are opening. A quick-service restaurant may benefit from high vehicle and pedestrian traffic, while a destination restaurant may be able to operate successfully in a less visible location.

Think about where your target customers live, work, shop, or spend their free time. The location should make it convenient for them to visit your restaurant.

📄 Review the Lease Before Signing

A restaurant lease can become one of your largest long-term business expenses. Review the lease carefully and understand the total occupancy cost before committing to the property.

  • Base rent and additional rent
  • Lease length and renewal options
  • Scheduled rent increases
  • Utilities and other property-related costs
  • Responsibility for repairs and maintenance
  • Signage and exterior advertising rights
  • Assignment or transfer conditions

⚖️ Check Zoning, Permits, and Property Requirements

Before committing to a property, confirm that the location can legally be used for your intended restaurant concept. Depending on the location, you may need to consider zoning, building requirements, health requirements, fire safety, occupancy limits, and other approvals.

Do not assume that a previous restaurant's approvals or setup will automatically apply to your new business. Verify the requirements with the appropriate local authorities before making major financial commitments.

🔎 Visit the Location at Different Times

A location can look very different depending on the time and day. Visit the property during lunch, dinner, evenings, and weekends when possible.

Observe traffic, parking, nearby businesses, pedestrian activity, noise, safety, and how easy it is for customers to find and access the property.

⚠️ Don't Choose a Location Based Only on Cheap Rent

A lower rent does not always mean a better business opportunity. A cheaper location with poor visibility, limited parking, low customer traffic, or the wrong customer base may generate much lower sales.

Compare the total cost of the location with its potential to generate sales. The goal is not simply to find the cheapest property, but to find a location that gives the restaurant a realistic opportunity to succeed.

🍳 4. Equipment and Supplies

Your equipment requirements will depend on your menu, restaurant concept, kitchen size, expected customer volume, and available space. Make a complete equipment list before purchasing anything so you can compare costs and avoid unnecessary expenses.

Common Restaurant Equipment

Depending on your restaurant concept, you may need equipment such as:

  • Cooking equipment such as ranges, ovens, grills, fryers, and cooking appliances
  • Refrigeration and freezer equipment
  • Food preparation equipment
  • Ventilation and exhaust systems
  • Dishwashing and sanitation equipment
  • Shelving, storage, and food storage containers
  • Smallwares such as pots, pans, knives, utensils, and cutting boards
  • Serving equipment, dishes, glassware, and tableware
  • POS terminals, printers, and payment equipment
  • Cleaning and sanitation supplies

🍽️ Let Your Menu Determine Your Equipment

Avoid buying equipment simply because it is commonly used in restaurants. Your menu should determine what equipment you actually need.

Review each menu item and identify the equipment, preparation space, storage, and cooking process required to produce it. This can help you avoid purchasing equipment that will rarely be used.

💰 New vs. Used Equipment

Depending on your budget and the type of equipment, buying quality used equipment may reduce your startup costs. However, used equipment should be inspected carefully before purchase.

  • Check the overall condition and age of the equipment
  • Confirm that the equipment works properly
  • Consider possible repair and replacement costs
  • Check whether any warranty or service coverage remains
  • Confirm that installation requirements can be met

📐 Check Your Space and Installation Requirements

Before purchasing large equipment, make sure it will fit through the building entrances and into the planned location. Also consider electrical, gas, plumbing, ventilation, drainage, and other installation requirements.

For major equipment and kitchen systems, confirm the installation requirements with qualified professionals before making the purchase.

📊 Plan for Your Expected Volume

Equipment should be appropriate for the amount of food and number of customers you expect to serve. Equipment that is too small may slow down service, while equipment that is significantly larger than necessary can increase your startup and operating costs.

Consider both your expected opening volume and reasonable future growth when selecting major equipment.

🔧 Think About Maintenance and Repairs

Restaurant equipment is used frequently and can eventually require maintenance or repairs. Before purchasing equipment, consider its reliability, availability of replacement parts, service options, and expected maintenance costs.

⚠️ Avoid Buying More Than You Need

It can be tempting to purchase every piece of equipment recommended by suppliers or other restaurant owners. However, unused equipment takes up valuable space and ties up money that could be used for inventory, payroll, marketing, or working capital.

Create your equipment list from your actual menu and operating plan, compare several options, and prioritize equipment that directly supports your restaurant's daily operations.

👥 5. Hiring and Managing Staff

Your employees are an important part of your restaurant's daily operations and customer experience. Depending on the size and concept of your restaurant, you may need cooks, servers, cashiers, dishwashers, cleaners, managers, or other staff.

Labour costs should be included in your business budget from the beginning. Consider not only hourly wages or salaries, but also payroll costs, benefits where applicable, training, and other employment-related expenses.

Common Restaurant Roles

The roles you need will depend on your menu, service style, operating hours, and expected customer volume. Common roles include:

  • Restaurant manager or supervisor
  • Cooks and kitchen staff
  • Servers and front-of-house staff
  • Cashiers and counter staff
  • Dishwashers and kitchen helpers
  • Cleaning and maintenance staff
  • Delivery or order preparation staff

👤 Hire for the Jobs You Actually Need

Before hiring, identify the tasks that must be completed during each shift. This can help you determine which positions are necessary and avoid hiring more employees than the business can support.

Cross-training employees in multiple tasks can also provide flexibility when someone is absent or when customer demand changes throughout the day.

💰 Understand Your Total Labour Cost

Employee wages are only one part of the cost of having staff. Depending on your location and employment arrangements, you may also have payroll contributions, benefits, vacation pay, training, uniforms, meals, recruitment costs, and other employment expenses.

Include these costs when preparing your monthly operating budget so that your labour expenses are not underestimated.

🎓 Train Your Employees Properly

New employees should understand your restaurant's procedures, food preparation standards, customer service expectations, cleaning requirements, safety procedures, and workplace rules.

Good training helps create consistency and can reduce mistakes, waste, customer complaints, and unnecessary staff turnover.

🕐 Plan Your Staff Schedule Carefully

Staffing levels should match your expected customer demand. You may need more employees during busy periods and fewer employees during slower periods.

Review your sales patterns over time and adjust schedules when necessary. Avoid scheduling too many employees during slow periods while making sure busy shifts have enough staff to provide good service.

📋 Create Clear Workplace Policies

Establish clear expectations for attendance, punctuality, dress code, customer service, food handling, cleaning, workplace conduct, and other important procedures.

Keep important employee information and workplace procedures organized so managers and employees know what is expected.

🤝 Keep Good Employees

Hiring and training new employees takes time and money. Creating a respectful workplace, providing proper training, communicating clearly, and recognizing good performance can help improve employee retention.

A stable and experienced team can improve efficiency, customer service, food quality, and the overall operation of the restaurant.

⚖️ Follow Employment Requirements

Restaurant owners are responsible for following the employment, payroll, workplace safety, minimum wage, scheduling, and other applicable requirements in their jurisdiction.

Requirements can vary by location and may change over time. Check with the appropriate government authorities or qualified professionals to make sure your business is meeting its obligations.

⚠️ Don't Understaff Your Restaurant

Reducing labour costs by having too few employees can create long wait times, poor customer service, employee burnout, mistakes, and lower food quality.

The goal is not simply to have the fewest employees possible. The goal is to build a staffing plan that provides good service while keeping labour costs appropriate for the restaurant's sales and operating model.

📣 6. Marketing Your Restaurant

A good restaurant needs customers to survive and grow. Your marketing plan should help people discover your restaurant, understand what you offer, choose to visit, and come back again.

Start planning your marketing before opening rather than waiting until the restaurant is ready. Building awareness early can help create interest and give your business a stronger start.

Build Your Restaurant Brand

Your restaurant's name, logo, menu, signage, website, photos, and overall appearance should create a consistent identity that matches your concept and target customers.

  • Choose a clear and memorable restaurant name
  • Create a professional and recognizable logo
  • Keep your menu design consistent with your restaurant concept
  • Use high-quality photos of your food and restaurant
  • Make your exterior signage easy to see and understand

🌐 Establish an Online Presence

Many customers search online before deciding where to eat. Make it easy for potential customers to find accurate information about your restaurant.

  • Create a simple and mobile-friendly website
  • Maintain accurate business listings and contact information
  • Keep your opening hours up to date
  • Make your current menu easy to find online
  • Clearly show your address and location
  • Provide an easy way for customers to contact you

📱 Use Social Media Strategically

Social media can help restaurants show their food, atmosphere, promotions, and personality. You do not need to be active on every platform. Focus on the channels your target customers actually use.

Consider sharing food photos, new menu items, behind-the-scenes content, special offers, customer experiences, events, and other content that gives people a reason to visit.

🎉 Plan Your Opening

Your opening can create an important first impression. Consider announcing the opening in advance and giving potential customers a reason to try the restaurant.

  • Announce your opening date in advance
  • Consider an opening promotion or special offer
  • Introduce the restaurant to nearby businesses and residents
  • Share opening updates through your online channels

🎁 Use Promotions Carefully

Promotions can attract new customers, but discounts should be planned carefully. A promotion that generates sales but loses money may not be good for the business.

Before offering a discount, understand the cost of the food, labour, and other expenses involved. Make sure the promotion supports your overall business goals.

⭐ Encourage Customer Reviews

Positive customer reviews can help build trust with people who are considering visiting your restaurant for the first time. Make it easy for satisfied customers to leave honest feedback.

Monitor your reviews and respond professionally to both positive and negative feedback. Use legitimate customer feedback to identify areas where the business can improve.

🔄 Focus on Repeat Customers

Getting a customer to return can be just as important as attracting a new customer. Consistent food quality, good service, reasonable value, and a positive experience can encourage customers to come back.

Consider loyalty programs, special offers for returning customers, seasonal menu items, or other appropriate ways to maintain a relationship with your customers.

📍 Market to Your Local Community

Restaurants often depend heavily on their surrounding community. Consider building relationships with nearby businesses, offices, residents, community organizations, and local events where appropriate.

📊 Track What Actually Works

Do not assume that every marketing activity is producing results. Track where customers are coming from and compare the cost of your marketing with the sales or customer activity it generates.

  • Track the results of promotions
  • Monitor engagement from social media
  • Review the results of paid advertising
  • Ask customers how they heard about the restaurant

⚠️ Don't Depend on Marketing to Fix a Bad Customer Experience

Marketing can bring people through the door, but the quality of the food, service, cleanliness, atmosphere, and overall experience will influence whether they return.

The strongest restaurant marketing strategy combines attracting new customers with providing an experience that gives existing customers a reason to return.

⚠️ 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many restaurant problems can be reduced through careful planning. Understanding common mistakes before opening can help you make better decisions and protect your money, time, and reputation.

💰 Financial Mistakes

  • Underestimating startup costs
  • Underestimating monthly operating expenses
  • Failing to maintain enough working capital
  • Spending too much money before the restaurant opens
  • Failing to monitor cash flow

📍 Location Mistakes

  • Choosing a location without enough research
  • Choosing a location based only on cheap rent
  • Ignoring parking, access, or visibility
  • Choosing a location that does not match the target customers
  • Signing a lease without fully understanding the costs and conditions

🍽️ Menu Mistakes

  • Creating a menu that is too large
  • Pricing menu items without understanding their actual costs
  • Ignoring food waste and portion control
  • Offering menu items that are unnecessarily complicated to prepare

👥 Staffing Mistakes

  • Ignoring labour and operating costs
  • Hiring more employees than the business can support
  • Operating with too few employees during busy periods
  • Failing to properly train employees
  • Creating inefficient staff schedules

⚙️ Operational Mistakes

  • Failing to monitor inventory and food costs
  • Buying unnecessary equipment
  • Ignoring equipment maintenance
  • Failing to maintain proper cleaning and sanitation procedures
  • Operating without clear procedures and responsibilities

⭐ Customer Experience Mistakes

  • Ignoring customer service problems
  • Allowing food quality to become inconsistent
  • Neglecting cleanliness and restaurant appearance
  • Ignoring customer reviews and feedback
  • Focusing only on new customers instead of encouraging repeat business

📊 Management Mistakes

  • Failing to keep accurate financial records
  • Not tracking important business numbers
  • Spending money on marketing without measuring results
  • Refusing to adjust the business when customer demand changes
  • Trying to manage everything without building reliable systems

💡 Learn From Your Numbers

One of the most important habits for a restaurant owner is regularly reviewing the business numbers. Monitor sales, food costs, labour costs, operating expenses, cash flow, customer feedback, and other important measures.

Problems are usually easier to correct when they are identified early. Regularly reviewing your results can help you identify what is working, what needs improvement, and where the business may be losing money.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start a restaurant?

There is no single startup amount that applies to every restaurant. Costs depend heavily on the concept, location, size, equipment, renovations, and staffing requirements.

Is buying an existing restaurant easier?

Buying an existing restaurant can reduce some setup work, but you should carefully review the lease, equipment, financial records, expenses, licences, and condition of the business before making a decision.
