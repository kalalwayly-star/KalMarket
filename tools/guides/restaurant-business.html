🍔 Starting a Restaurant Business

Starting a restaurant can be an exciting business opportunity, but it requires careful planning, budgeting, and preparation. This guide walks you through the major steps involved in starting and operating a restaurant.

Restaurant Business Guide

Thinking about starting a restaurant? This guide covers the important areas you should consider before opening, from planning and startup costs to staffing, marketing, and day-to-day operations.

📋 1. Plan Your Restaurant

Before spending money on equipment or signing a lease, decide what type of restaurant you want to operate.

  • Choose your restaurant concept.
  • Decide what food and drinks you will offer.
  • Identify your target customers.
  • Research your local competition.
  • Create an initial business budget.

💰 2. Understand Your Startup Costs

Restaurant startup costs can vary significantly depending on the location, size, concept, equipment, renovations, and whether you are opening a new location or taking over an existing restaurant.

Before signing a lease or making major purchases, create a detailed startup budget. Try to estimate each expense separately rather than using one general startup-cost number.

Common Startup Expenses

Some common one-time or initial expenses include:

  • Lease and security deposit
  • Renovations and improvements
  • Kitchen equipment
  • Furniture and fixtures
  • POS and payment systems
  • Initial food and beverage inventory
  • Licences and permits
  • Insurance
  • Professional and legal services
  • Website, branding, and signage
  • Marketing and advertising
  • Initial staff hiring and training
  • Initial working capital

📊 Don't Forget Monthly Operating Costs

Startup costs are only part of the financial picture. You also need to plan for the regular expenses required to operate the restaurant after opening.

Common monthly operating costs may include:

  • Rent and occupancy expenses
  • Food and beverage costs
  • Employee wages and payroll costs
  • Utilities
  • Insurance
  • Cleaning and maintenance
  • Software and payment processing
  • Marketing and advertising
  • Accounting and professional services
  • Equipment repairs and replacement
  • Supplies and packaging
  • Delivery and other service fees

💡 Plan for Unexpected Expenses

Opening a restaurant can involve unexpected expenses such as construction delays, equipment problems, higher renovation costs, permit delays, or lower-than-expected sales during the first few months.

Having working capital available can help the business handle these situations without putting unnecessary pressure on cash flow.

It's also a good idea to include a contingency amount in your startup budget for unexpected costs. The exact amount will depend on the size and complexity of the project, but the important point is to avoid using every dollar of your available capital before opening.

💰 Don't Spend Your Entire Budget Before Opening

One common mistake is using most of the available money on renovations, equipment, and furniture before the restaurant opens.

A restaurant may need cash after opening to cover payroll, inventory, rent, utilities, marketing, repairs, and other expenses while sales are still developing.

Your startup budget should therefore answer two separate questions:

  1. How much money do I need to open?
  2. How much money do I need to operate until the business becomes stable?

That second question is extremely important for a new restaurant.

📍 3. Choose the Right Location

Location can have a major effect on a restaurant's success. A good location should match your target customers, concept, budget, and expected sales. Before signing a lease, carefully evaluate the area and the property itself.

Important Location Factors

Consider the following factors when comparing potential locations:

  • Visibility and signage from the street
  • Customer and vehicle traffic
  • Parking availability and accessibility
  • The number and type of potential customers in the area
  • Nearby restaurants and competing businesses
  • Rent and other occupancy costs
  • Access to public transportation and major roads
  • Nearby offices, residential areas, schools, hotels, or attractions
  • Local population and customer demographics
  • Safety and overall condition of the surrounding area

👥 Make Sure the Location Matches Your Customers

The best location depends on the type of restaurant you are opening. A quick-service restaurant may benefit from high vehicle and pedestrian traffic, while a destination restaurant may be able to operate successfully in a less visible location.

Think about where your target customers live, work, shop, or spend their free time. The location should make it convenient for them to visit your restaurant.

📄 Review the Lease Before Signing

A restaurant lease can become one of your largest long-term business expenses. Review the lease carefully and understand the total occupancy cost before committing to the property.

  • Base rent and additional rent
  • Lease length and renewal options
  • Scheduled rent increases
  • Utilities and other property-related costs
  • Responsibility for repairs and maintenance
  • Signage and exterior advertising rights
  • Assignment or transfer conditions

⚖️ Check Zoning, Permits, and Property Requirements

Before committing to a property, confirm that the location can legally be used for your intended restaurant concept. Depending on the location, you may need to consider zoning, building requirements, health requirements, fire safety, occupancy limits, and other approvals.

Do not assume that a previous restaurant's approvals or setup will automatically apply to your new business. Verify the requirements with the appropriate local authorities before making major financial commitments.

🔎 Visit the Location at Different Times

A location can look very different depending on the time and day. Visit the property during lunch, dinner, evenings, and weekends when possible.

Observe traffic, parking, nearby businesses, pedestrian activity, noise, safety, and how easy it is for customers to find and access the property.

⚠️ Don't Choose a Location Based Only on Cheap Rent

A lower rent does not always mean a better business opportunity. A cheaper location with poor visibility, limited parking, low customer traffic, or the wrong customer base may generate much lower sales.

Compare the total cost of the location with its potential to generate sales. The goal is not simply to find the cheapest property, but to find a location that gives the restaurant a realistic opportunity to succeed.

🍳 4. Equipment and Supplies

Your equipment requirements will depend on your menu, restaurant concept, kitchen size, expected customer volume, and available space. Make a complete equipment list before purchasing anything so you can compare costs and avoid unnecessary expenses.

Common Restaurant Equipment

Depending on your restaurant concept, you may need equipment such as:

  • Cooking equipment such as ranges, ovens, grills, fryers, and cooking appliances
  • Refrigeration and freezer equipment
  • Food preparation equipment
  • Ventilation and exhaust systems
  • Dishwashing and sanitation equipment
  • Shelving, storage, and food storage containers
  • Smallwares such as pots, pans, knives, utensils, and cutting boards
  • Serving equipment, dishes, glassware, and tableware
  • POS terminals, printers, and payment equipment
  • Cleaning and sanitation supplies

🍽️ Let Your Menu Determine Your Equipment

Avoid buying equipment simply because it is commonly used in restaurants. Your menu should determine what equipment you actually need.

Review each menu item and identify the equipment, preparation space, storage, and cooking process required to produce it. This can help you avoid purchasing equipment that will rarely be used.

💰 New vs. Used Equipment

Depending on your budget and the type of equipment, buying quality used equipment may reduce your startup costs. However, used equipment should be inspected carefully before purchase.

  • Check the overall condition and age of the equipment
  • Confirm that the equipment works properly
  • Consider possible repair and replacement costs
  • Check whether any warranty or service coverage remains
  • Confirm that installation requirements can be met

📐 Check Your Space and Installation Requirements

Before purchasing large equipment, make sure it will fit through the building entrances and into the planned location. Also consider electrical, gas, plumbing, ventilation, drainage, and other installation requirements.

For major equipment and kitchen systems, confirm the installation requirements with qualified professionals before making the purchase.

📊 Plan for Your Expected Volume

Equipment should be appropriate for the amount of food and number of customers you expect to serve. Equipment that is too small may slow down service, while equipment that is significantly larger than necessary can increase your startup and operating costs.

Consider both your expected opening volume and reasonable future growth when selecting major equipment.

🔧 Think About Maintenance and Repairs

Restaurant equipment is used frequently and can eventually require maintenance or repairs. Before purchasing equipment, consider its reliability, availability of replacement parts, service options, and expected maintenance costs.

⚠️ Avoid Buying More Than You Need

It can be tempting to purchase every piece of equipment recommended by suppliers or other restaurant owners. However, unused equipment takes up valuable space and ties up money that could be used for inventory, payroll, marketing, or working capital.

Create your equipment list from your actual menu and operating plan, compare several options, and prioritize equipment that directly supports your restaurant's daily operations.

👥 5. Hiring Staff

Depending on the size and concept of your restaurant, you may need cooks, servers, cashiers, cleaners, managers, or other staff. Labour costs should be included in your business budget from the beginning.

📣 6. Marketing Your Restaurant

A good restaurant needs customers to survive. Consider building a website, maintaining accurate business listings, using social media, offering promotions, and encouraging satisfied customers to return.

⚠️ 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underestimating startup costs
  • Choosing a location without enough research
  • Creating a menu that is too large
  • Ignoring labour and operating costs
  • Failing to maintain enough working capital
  • Spending too much on renovations before testing the concept

✅ Restaurant Startup Checklist

  • Choose a restaurant concept
  • Research the local market
  • Create a business plan
  • Estimate startup and monthly costs
  • Find a suitable location
  • Research required licences and permits
  • Arrange financing if needed
  • Purchase equipment and supplies
  • Hire and train staff
  • Prepare your marketing plan
  • Complete final inspections and preparations
  • Open your restaurant

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start a restaurant?

There is no single startup amount that applies to every restaurant. Costs depend heavily on the concept, location, size, equipment, renovations, and staffing requirements.

Is buying an existing restaurant easier?

Buying an existing restaurant can reduce some setup work, but you should carefully review the lease, equipment, financial records, expenses, licences, and condition of the business before making a decision.
