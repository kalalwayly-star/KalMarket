Study in Canada

A practical guide to choosing a school, getting accepted, applying for a study permit, preparing financially, and starting your life as an international student in Canada.

⚠️ Important Information

Canadian immigration rules, study permit requirements, fees, financial requirements, and processing procedures can change. This guide provides general educational information and is not legal immigration advice. Always verify current requirements with the Government of Canada before applying.

🎓 What Is a Canadian Study Permit?

A study permit is a document issued by the Government of Canada that allows eligible foreign nationals to study at a designated learning institution in Canada.

Most foreign nationals who want to study in Canada for more than a short period need a study permit. The permit normally includes conditions that you must follow while studying in Canada.

A study permit is not the same thing as a visa or electronic travel authorization. Depending on your citizenship and circumstances, you may also need the appropriate travel document to enter Canada.

✅ Who Can Apply for a Study Permit?

To qualify for a study permit, you generally need to meet several requirements established by Canadian immigration authorities.

You Generally Need to

  • Be accepted by a designated learning institution (DLI)
  • Prove that you have enough money to support yourself and accompanying family members
  • Obey Canadian laws and meet admissibility requirements
  • Meet health requirements when a medical exam is required
  • Satisfy an officer that you will leave Canada when your authorized stay ends, when required

🏫 Step 1 — Choose the Right School

Choosing the right school and program is one of the most important steps in your study plan. Before applying, make sure the institution is a designated learning institution (DLI) and that the program matches your education and career goals.

Check These Things Before Applying

  • Confirm that the school is on the official DLI list
  • Review the exact program and credential
  • Compare tuition and other school fees
  • Check the program length and start date
  • Consider the city, transportation, housing, and living costs
  • Check language requirements
  • Check whether the program may qualify for post-graduation work opportunities

🚨 Be Careful With School and Immigration Scams

Verify schools and programs through official Canadian sources. Be careful with anyone who guarantees a study permit, permanent residence, or a job simply because you enroll in a program.

🏫 What Is a DLI?

A designated learning institution (DLI) is a Canadian school approved by a provincial or territorial government to host international students.

You should confirm that your institution and program meet the current requirements before submitting a study permit application.

Not every program at every DLI necessarily leads to eligibility for a post-graduation work permit. Always check the current rules for the specific school and program.

📝 Step 2 — Apply to the School

Each school has its own admission requirements. Apply directly through the institution and follow its instructions carefully.

Schools May Ask for

  • Passport or identity information
  • School or university transcripts
  • Diplomas or certificates
  • English or French language test results when required
  • Resume or work experience information
  • Reference letters when required
  • Personal statement or letter of intent when required

📩 Step 3 — Get Your Letter of Acceptance

After the school accepts your application, it normally provides a Letter of Acceptance (LOA). This document is an important part of your study permit application.

Check Your Letter Carefully

  • Your name and personal information
  • Program name
  • Tuition information
  • Program start and expected completion dates
  • Registration information or deadline

For post-secondary applications, the institution may need to validate the Letter of Acceptance with IRCC. Follow your school's instructions carefully.

📜 Step 4 — PAL or TAL

In most cases, international students need a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) or Territorial Attestation Letter (TAL) from the province or territory where they plan to study.

The designated learning institution generally provides instructions about how to obtain the attestation letter.

Important Exceptions

Some students are exempt from the PAL/TAL requirement. For example, beginning January 1, 2026, students entering eligible degree-granting master's or doctoral programs at public DLIs may be exempt. Other exemptions may also apply.

Make sure your PAL or TAL is valid when you submit your study permit application.

⚜️ If You Plan to Study in Quebec

Quebec has its own immigration and study requirements. Students who plan to study in Quebec generally need to obtain a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) when required.

Quebec also has its own financial requirements. Check the current Quebec government requirements before applying.

Do not assume that the requirements for another province apply to Quebec.

💰 Step 5 — Prove You Can Afford to Study in Canada

You must show that you have enough money to pay for your studies and support yourself and any accompanying family members without relying on unauthorized work.

You Need to Plan for

  • Tuition fees
  • Living expenses
  • Transportation to and from Canada
  • Living expenses and transportation for accompanying family members
  • Housing, food, insurance, transportation, textbooks, clothing, and other personal costs

Common Proof of Funds

  • Bank statements
  • Proof of tuition or housing payments
  • Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) when applicable
  • Student or education loan
  • Scholarship or financial award
  • Financial support from another person with supporting evidence

⚠️ Important

The required living-expense amount is updated periodically. Check the current Government of Canada amount before applying. Tuition and transportation costs are additional.

📄 Step 6 — Prepare Your Documents

Your personalized document checklist depends on your circumstances. Prepare as many required documents as possible before submitting your application.

Common Documents

  • Letter of Acceptance from your DLI
  • PAL/TAL when required
  • Valid passport or travel document
  • Required photographs
  • Proof of financial support
  • Letter of explanation
  • Education records and transcripts
  • Language test results when applicable
  • Medical examination documents when required
  • Police certificate when required
  • Family documents when family members are accompanying you

📝 Letter of Explanation / Study Plan

A clear letter of explanation can help an officer understand your study plans, background, and reasons for choosing your program.

You Can Explain

  • Your education and work background
  • Why you selected the specific program
  • Why you selected the school
  • Why studying in Canada makes sense for your educational or career plans
  • Your plans after completing your studies
  • How your education and living expenses will be financed

Keep your explanation truthful, consistent, and supported by your documents.

💻 Step 7 — Apply for the Study Permit

Most study permit applications must be submitted online. Use the official Government of Canada application system and follow the personalized document checklist it provides.

Basic Application Process

  1. Receive your Letter of Acceptance.
  2. Obtain your PAL/TAL or prove that you qualify for an exemption.
  3. Obtain a CAQ if you are studying in Quebec and it is required.
  4. Prepare your financial and supporting documents.
  5. Complete the online study permit application.
  6. Upload your documents.
  7. Pay the required fees.
  8. Provide biometrics if required.
  9. Complete a medical examination if required.
  10. Respond to any additional document or information requests.
  11. Wait for the decision.

💳 Study Permit Fees

The Government of Canada currently lists the study permit processing fee as CAN$150. Other costs, such as biometrics, medical examinations, translations, language tests, tuition, and travel, may also apply depending on your situation.

Government fees can change. Always verify current fees on the official Canada.ca website before submitting your application.

🧬 Step 8 — Biometrics

Some study permit applicants must provide fingerprints and a photograph after submitting their application.

If you receive a biometrics instruction letter, follow the instructions and complete your biometrics as soon as possible to avoid unnecessary delays.

🩺 Medical Examination

Depending on your circumstances, you may need a medical examination before or during the processing of your application.

Medical requirements can depend on factors such as how long you plan to stay in Canada, where you have lived or travelled, and the type of work you may perform.

If a medical examination is required, follow IRCC instructions and use an approved panel physician.

⏳ Processing Your Application

Processing times vary depending on where you apply, the type of application, whether your application is complete, and other factors.

Check the current official processing time before making travel arrangements or leaving your current school or job.

An incomplete application can cause delays or may be returned without processing.

🎉 If Your Study Permit Is Approved

Read the approval instructions carefully. Depending on where you applied, you may receive instructions about obtaining the documents you need to travel to Canada.

Before Travelling, Check

  • Passport validity
  • Required visa or eTA
  • Study permit approval documents
  • Letter of Acceptance
  • Financial documents
  • Housing arrangements
  • Health insurance arrangements

🛬 Arriving in Canada

When you arrive in Canada, a border services officer may review your passport, travel documents, study permit documents, Letter of Acceptance, and other information.

Keep These Documents With You

  • Passport
  • Study permit approval documents
  • Letter of Acceptance
  • Proof of financial support
  • Canadian address or housing information

Check the study permit issued to you and make sure the personal information, dates, and conditions are correct.

📚 Your Responsibilities as a Student

Having a study permit comes with conditions. International students must follow the conditions of their permit and Canadian immigration rules.

  • Stay enrolled at a designated learning institution when required
  • Make reasonable progress toward completing your program
  • Follow the conditions listed on your study permit
  • Maintain valid immigration status
  • Keep your personal information updated when required

💼 Can International Students Work?

Eligible international students may be allowed to work while studying, subject to the conditions of their study permit and current Canadian rules.

Before Working, Check

  • The work conditions printed on your study permit
  • Whether your program and student status make you eligible to work
  • The current limits and rules governing student work
  • Whether you need a Social Insurance Number (SIN)

Do not assume that every international student has the same work authorization. Check the current rules before accepting employment.

🔄 Changing Schools or Programs

If you change your designated learning institution or program, you may have additional immigration requirements. Do not assume that you can simply change schools without checking the current rules.

Before changing schools, contact your new school and check the current IRCC requirements for changing your institution or program.

⏰ Extending Your Study Permit

If you need more time to complete your studies, you may need to extend your study permit before it expires.

Do not wait until the last minute. Review the current IRCC instructions and apply early enough to protect your legal status.

⚠️ If Your Study Permit Has Expired

If your study permit has expired and you want to continue studying in Canada, you may need to restore your student status and apply for a new study permit. Check the current IRCC requirements immediately.

👨‍👩‍👧 Bringing Your Family to Canada

Depending on your situation, your spouse or common-law partner and dependent children may be able to accompany you to Canada.

Family members may need their own applications, passports, supporting documents, and proof of relationship.

The ability of a spouse or partner to obtain a work permit depends on current eligibility rules. Do not assume that every student's spouse automatically qualifies.

👦 Studying in Canada as a Minor

Children and teenagers studying in Canada can have additional requirements depending on their age, school level, travel arrangements, and who is accompanying them.

Children under certain ages who are travelling without a parent or legal guardian may need a custodian in Canada.

Parents and guardians should check the current requirements before making travel arrangements.

🎓 After Graduation — What Comes Next?

Some graduates of eligible programs may qualify for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP), allowing them to gain Canadian work experience after completing their studies.

PGWP eligibility is not automatic. It depends on the school, program, study status, program length, and the rules in effect when you apply.

Before choosing a program, check whether the specific institution and program meet the current PGWP requirements.

🏠 Can Studying in Canada Lead to Permanent Residence?

Canadian education and work experience may help some people qualify for permanent residence programs, but studying in Canada does not automatically lead to permanent residence.

Your options may depend on your education, Canadian work experience, language ability, occupation, age, and other factors.

⚠️ Common Study Permit Mistakes

  • Submitting an incomplete application
  • Providing weak or unexplained financial documentation
  • Providing information that is inconsistent across documents
  • Choosing a school or program without checking its current eligibility
  • Forgetting the PAL/TAL requirement when it applies
  • Ignoring Quebec-specific requirements when studying in Quebec
  • Providing a weak or unclear study plan
  • Allowing a study permit to expire without taking action
  • Working without confirming that you are authorized to work

🚨 Beware of Education and Immigration Scams

Be careful with anyone who promises guaranteed admission, a guaranteed study permit, guaranteed permanent residence, or a guaranteed job in Canada in exchange for money.

  • Guaranteed visa or study permit promises
  • Fake schools or fake admission letters
  • Requests to submit false documents
  • Unexplained large payments
  • Promises that studying a particular program automatically gives permanent residence

Verify schools, programs, immigration information, and representatives through official sources before paying money or submitting personal documents.
