Work in Canada

Learn about Canadian work permits, who may need one, how to apply, and what you should know before working in Canada.

⚠️ Important Information

Canadian immigration and work permit requirements can change. This guide provides general educational information and is not legal immigration advice. Always verify current requirements with the Government of Canada before applying.

👷 Who Needs a Work Permit?

Many foreign nationals need a work permit to legally work in Canada. A work permit is generally required when a person wants to work in Canada temporarily.

There are some situations where a person may be allowed to work without a work permit. The rules depend on the type of work, the person's status, and the specific circumstances.

Always check the current official requirements before starting work in Canada.

🛂 Main Types of Work Permits

🏢

Employer-Specific Work Permit

This type of permit allows you to work under specific conditions, such as the employer, occupation, location, and duration listed on the permit.

🔓

Open Work Permit

An open work permit may allow you to work for most employers in Canada, subject to the conditions on the permit and applicable rules.

🏢 Employer-Specific Work Permit

An employer-specific work permit normally identifies the employer you are authorized to work for and may include conditions related to your occupation, location, and period of work.

You May Need

  • A genuine job offer from a Canadian employer
  • An LMIA or another form of authorization when required
  • Employment information or contract
  • Supporting documents required by IRCC

📋 What Is an LMIA?

A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is a document that a Canadian employer may need before hiring a temporary foreign worker.

An LMIA is generally used to assess whether hiring a temporary foreign worker is expected to have a positive or neutral impact on Canada's labour market.

Not every work permit requires an LMIA. Some work permits are issued under programs or situations that are exempt from the LMIA requirement.

An employer should not ask a worker to pay the employer's LMIA fee when that fee is the employer's responsibility.

🔓 Open Work Permits

An open work permit may allow an eligible person to work for most employers in Canada without needing a job-specific work permit.

Some Situations May Include

  • Certain spouses or partners of workers or international students
  • Certain international graduates
  • Certain applicants connected to permanent residence programs
  • Other situations specifically authorized under Canadian immigration programs

Eligibility for an open work permit depends on the specific immigration program and your personal circumstances.

🔎 Finding a Job in Canada

If you need an employer-specific work permit, you will generally need a genuine job offer from a Canadian employer before applying.

Tips for Finding Legitimate Employment

  • Prepare a Canadian-style resume
  • Highlight your education, skills, and work experience
  • Research the employer before accepting an offer
  • Carefully review the written employment offer
  • Be careful with employers who ask for money in exchange for a job

📋 Work Permit Eligibility

Eligibility depends on the type of work permit and the immigration program you are applying under.

You May Need to Show

  • A valid passport or travel document
  • A genuine job offer when required
  • Enough money to support yourself and your family when required
  • Your intention to leave Canada when your authorized stay ends, when applicable
  • Compliance with applicable admissibility requirements

📄 Documents You May Need

The documents required depend on the type of work permit and your personal situation.

  • Valid passport or travel document
  • Work permit application forms
  • Job offer or employment contract when required
  • LMIA documents when required
  • Employer information
  • Resume or curriculum vitae
  • Proof of work experience
  • Education or professional credentials when applicable
  • Proof of financial support when required

Never provide false information or fraudulent documents in an immigration application.

🧬 Biometrics

Some work permit applicants need to provide fingerprints and a photograph as part of the application process.

If biometrics are required, IRCC will provide instructions about when and where to provide them.

📝 How to Apply for a Work Permit

  1. Determine which type of work permit you may be eligible for.
  2. Determine whether your employer needs an LMIA.
  3. Obtain the required job offer or employment documents.
  4. Gather your supporting documents.
  5. Complete the appropriate IRCC application.
  6. Pay the required government fees.
  7. Provide biometrics if required.
  8. Respond to requests for additional information or documents.
  9. Wait for a decision.

💳 Work Permit Fees

Government fees may apply to a work permit application. Additional fees may apply depending on the type of permit or application.

Fees can change. Always check the official Government of Canada website for current fees before submitting your application.

⏳ Processing Times

Work permit processing times vary depending on the type of application, where you apply from, your circumstances, and other factors.

Processing times are estimates and are not guarantees. Check the current IRCC processing information before making important plans.

✅ After Your Work Permit Is Approved

If your application is approved, carefully review the instructions provided by IRCC before travelling to Canada or starting work.

Check Your Work Permit

  • Employer name, if applicable
  • Occupation or job conditions
  • Work location, if applicable
  • Start and expiry dates
  • Any additional conditions listed on the permit

🛬 Arriving in Canada to Work

When you arrive in Canada, a border services officer may review your documents and determine whether you can enter Canada.

Keep Important Documents Available

  • Passport or travel document
  • Work permit approval documents
  • Job offer or employment information
  • Other supporting documents requested by IRCC

Do not begin working until you are legally authorized to work in Canada.

🔄 Can You Change Jobs?

Whether you can change employers depends on the type and conditions of your work permit.

If you have an employer-specific work permit, you generally cannot simply begin working for a different employer without obtaining the required authorization.

Open work permit holders generally have more flexibility to change employers, subject to the conditions listed on their permit.

⏰ Extending Your Work Permit

If you want to continue working in Canada after your current work permit expires, you may need to apply to extend or change your work permit before it expires.

Do not wait until the last moment. Check the current IRCC requirements and submit your application before your authorization expires when applicable.

⚠️ Common Work Permit Problems

  • Fake or questionable job offers
  • Missing or incomplete documents
  • Not meeting the requirements of the program
  • Problems with an LMIA when one is required
  • Insufficient financial documentation when required
  • False information or misrepresentation
  • Allowing a work permit to expire without taking appropriate action

🚨 Beware of Job and Immigration Scams

Be extremely careful with people who promise a guaranteed Canadian job, work permit, or immigration approval in exchange for money.

  • Guaranteed work permit promises
  • Fake Canadian job offers
  • Requests to buy an LMIA
  • Large upfront payments for a supposed job
  • Requests for fake documents

Verify employers, job offers, immigration representatives, and government information before paying money or submitting documents.
