Visit Canada

Learn how to visit Canada, what documents you may need, how to apply, and what to expect when you arrive.

⚠️ Important Information

Canadian immigration and travel requirements can change. This guide provides general educational information and is not legal immigration advice. Always verify current requirements with the Government of Canada before travelling or applying.

🇨🇦 Who Can Visit Canada?

People from many countries can visit Canada, but the documents required depend on your citizenship, travel document, method of travel, and personal circumstances.

Depending on your situation, you may need a visitor visa, an electronic travel authorization (eTA), or other documentation before travelling to Canada.

Having a visa or eTA does not automatically guarantee entry to Canada. A border services officer makes the final decision when you arrive.

🛂 Visitor Visa

A visitor visa, also called a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV), is a document placed in your passport that allows you to travel to a Canadian port of entry and request entry as a temporary visitor.

Common Reasons for Visiting

  • Tourism and vacations
  • Visiting family or friends
  • Certain business visits
  • Attending events or other temporary activities

A visitor visa does not normally give you permission to work in Canada. If you want to work, you may need a work permit.

✈️ Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA)

An electronic travel authorization (eTA) is an electronic travel document that may be required for certain visa-exempt foreign nationals travelling to Canada by air.

An eTA is different from a visitor visa. Whether you need an eTA depends on your citizenship, travel document, and method of travel.

Do not assume that you need an eTA simply because you are travelling to Canada. Check the official Government of Canada requirements for your specific situation.

📋 Basic Visitor Eligibility

To visit Canada temporarily, you generally need to satisfy an immigration officer that you meet the applicable requirements.

  • Have a valid travel document, such as a passport
  • Be in good health when required by the rules
  • Have enough money for your stay
  • Have a genuine temporary purpose for your visit
  • Have circumstances that support your intention to leave Canada at the end of your authorized stay
  • Meet applicable security and admissibility requirements

📄 Documents You May Need

The exact documents required depend on your circumstances and the type of application you are making.

  • Valid passport or travel document
  • Completed application and supporting forms
  • Required photographs
  • Proof of financial support
  • Employment or business documents
  • Travel plans or itinerary
  • Hotel or accommodation information
  • Invitation letter, when applicable
  • Proof of relationship, when visiting family
  • Previous travel or immigration history, when relevant

Do not submit false or altered documents. Providing false information or fraudulent documents can have serious immigration consequences.

💰 How Much Money Do You Need?

There is not one universal amount of money that applies to every visitor. The amount you may need depends on your individual circumstances.

Officers may consider the length of your stay, where you will stay, the number of people travelling, your planned activities, and who will pay for your expenses.

Common Travel Expenses

  • Flights
  • Accommodation
  • Food
  • Transportation
  • Activities and entertainment
  • Emergency or unexpected expenses

🏠 Invitation Letter

If you are visiting family or friends in Canada, an invitation letter may help explain the purpose of your visit and your relationship with the person you are visiting.

Information an Invitation Letter May Include

  • Name and contact information of the host
  • Relationship between the host and visitor
  • Purpose of the visit
  • Expected dates and length of the visit
  • Where the visitor will stay
  • Information about who will pay for expenses

An invitation letter does not guarantee that a visitor visa will be approved.

🧬 Biometrics

Some applicants need to provide biometrics as part of the Canadian immigration application process.

Biometrics generally include fingerprints and a photograph. If biometrics are required, IRCC will provide instructions about when and where to provide them.

Some applicants may be exempt from providing biometrics. Check the current official requirements for your situation.

📝 How to Apply

  1. Determine whether you need a visitor visa, eTA, or another travel document.
  2. Check the current eligibility requirements.
  3. Gather your supporting documents.
  4. Use the appropriate official IRCC application system.
  5. Complete your application accurately.
  6. Pay the required government fees.
  7. Provide biometrics if required.
  8. Respond to requests for additional documents or information if IRCC contacts you.
  9. Wait for a decision.
  10. If approved, follow the instructions provided by IRCC before travelling.

💳 Application Fees

Government application fees may apply for a visitor visa or biometrics. Fees can change, so always check the official Government of Canada website for the current amounts before paying.

⏳ Processing Times

Processing times vary depending on the type of application, where you apply from, whether your application is complete, and other factors.

Processing times are estimates and are not a guarantee. Check the current IRCC processing-time information before making travel plans.

🔍 What Happens After You Apply?

  1. IRCC receives your application.
  2. Your application is reviewed.
  3. Biometrics may be collected if required.
  4. IRCC may request additional documents, information, or an interview.
  5. IRCC makes a decision on your application.

If your application is refused, carefully read the decision letter to understand the reasons for the refusal and determine what options may be available to you.

🛬 Arriving at the Canadian Border

When you arrive in Canada, you will be examined by a border services officer. You may be asked questions about your trip and your plans.

You May Be Asked About

  • The purpose of your visit
  • How long you plan to stay
  • Where you will stay
  • Your available funds
  • Your plans to leave Canada
  • Goods and items you are bringing into Canada

A visitor visa or eTA does not guarantee entry. The border services officer determines whether you can enter Canada.

🕐 How Long Can a Visitor Stay?

The validity period of a visitor visa is not the same as the amount of time you are authorized to stay in Canada.

The border services officer determines how long you may stay when you enter Canada. In some cases, you may receive a visitor record with a specific departure date.

If you want to stay longer than your authorized period, you may need to apply for a visitor record before your current status expires.

Do not overstay your authorized period in Canada.

💼 Can Visitors Work in Canada?

A visitor visa or visitor status does not normally give you permission to work in Canada.

If your goal is to work in Canada, review the requirements for a Canadian work permit.

🎓 Can Visitors Study in Canada?

The rules for studying in Canada depend on the length and type of study. Some short-term studies may not require a study permit, while longer programs may require one.

If your main goal is to study in Canada, review the current study permit requirements.

👨‍👩‍👧 Visiting Family in Canada

If you are visiting relatives or friends in Canada, you may want to provide information about your relationship, your host, where you will stay, and how your trip will be paid for.

Parents and grandparents of eligible Canadian citizens or permanent residents may have another option called the Super Visa. This is different from an ordinary visitor visa and has its own eligibility requirements.

👶 Children Traveling to Canada

Children travelling to Canada need appropriate travel documents. Additional documents may be useful or required depending on whether a child is travelling alone, with one parent, or with another adult.

  • Child's passport or travel document
  • Birth certificate or proof of relationship
  • Consent letter when appropriate
  • Information about parents or legal guardians

⚠️ Common Reasons Applications Can Have Problems

There are many possible reasons an application may be refused or require additional information. Some common concerns include:

  • Insufficient evidence of financial support
  • Unclear or unsupported purpose of travel
  • Insufficient evidence that the applicant will leave Canada at the end of the visit
  • Incomplete application or missing documents
  • Contradictory or inconsistent information
  • Criminal, security, medical, or other admissibility concerns
  • False information or misrepresentation

🚨 Protect Yourself From Immigration Scams

Be careful with websites, individuals, or companies that promise guaranteed visas or guaranteed entry to Canada.

  • Fake Government of Canada websites
  • Guaranteed visa or approval promises
  • Fake Canadian job offers
  • Unusual or suspicious payment requests
  • Fake Canada government emails or messages

Always verify important immigration information through the official Government of Canada website.
