🎨 Start a House Painting Business

A house painting business can be started with relatively low startup costs and can serve homeowners, landlords, property managers, and businesses.

You can start by working alone, build a customer base, and gradually expand by adding employees, equipment, and larger projects.

1. Choose Your Painting Services

Start by deciding which painting services you want to provide. You can specialize in residential painting or offer several related services.

  • 🏠 Interior House Painting
  • 🏡 Exterior House Painting
  • 🛏️ Room Painting
  • 🪵 Doors, Trim & Baseboards
  • 🗄️ Cabinet Painting
  • 🌳 Deck & Fence Painting
  • 🏢 Small Commercial Painting

2. Understand Your Startup Costs

A painting business can often be started without a large investment, especially if you begin with smaller residential projects.

  • Paint and painting materials
  • Brushes, rollers, and tools
  • Ladders and access equipment
  • Vehicle or transportation
  • Business insurance
  • Business registration and licenses
  • Advertising and marketing
  • Scheduling and invoicing software

3. Equipment & Supplies

Start with the equipment required for your first projects. You can upgrade your equipment as your business grows.

  • Professional paint brushes
  • Paint rollers and trays
  • Ladders
  • Drop cloths and protective coverings
  • Painter's tape
  • Sanding tools
  • Safety equipment
💡 Start With the Basics

Purchase the equipment you need for your first projects instead of investing heavily before you have customers.

4. Set Your Prices

Your prices should cover labor, paint, supplies, transportation, insurance, overhead, and your desired profit.

💡 Simple Pricing Formula

Project Price = Labor + Materials + Travel + Overhead + Profit

You can price painting jobs by the hour, by the room, by the square foot, or by the entire project.

5. Calculate Your Profit

Revenue does not equal profit. Subtract all business expenses to understand how much your painting business is actually earning.

  • Total customer revenue
  • Employee or contractor costs
  • Paint and materials
  • Vehicle and fuel costs
  • Insurance
  • Marketing
  • Other operating expenses

6. Get Your First Customers

Your first customers can come from your local community, referrals, online listings, property owners, and relationships with local businesses.

  • Post your services on local marketplaces
  • Use social media
  • Ask satisfied customers for referrals
  • Build positive reviews
  • Contact real estate professionals
  • Contact landlords and property managers

7. Marketing Your Business

A professional appearance can help customers feel confident when choosing your painting company.

  • Create a professional business name and logo
  • Keep your vehicle clean and professional
  • Take before-and-after photos
  • Create an online presence
  • Encourage customer reviews

8. Insurance & Business Requirements

Depending on your location and the services you provide, you may need business registration, permits, licenses, insurance, or other requirements.

⚠️ Important

Requirements vary by location and type of work. Check the applicable government requirements and insurance needs before starting your business.

9. Hiring Employees

You can often start a painting business by yourself. As demand increases, hiring painters can allow you to complete more projects.

Before hiring, make sure you understand payroll, employment requirements, insurance, training, and workplace safety obligations.

10. Grow Your Business

Once you have consistent customers, you can grow by adding services, expanding your service area, hiring employees, and taking on larger projects.

  • Add additional painting services
  • Expand your service area
  • Hire additional painters
  • Invest in professional equipment
  • Target commercial customers
  • Build repeat customers

✅ House Painting Startup Checklist

  • Choose your painting services
  • Research local demand
  • Calculate startup costs
  • Purchase necessary equipment
  • Determine your pricing
  • Check licenses and requirements
  • Obtain appropriate insurance
  • Create your business brand
  • Start marketing
  • Get your first customers
  • Track revenue and expenses
  • Build repeat customers
  • Reinvest and grow
